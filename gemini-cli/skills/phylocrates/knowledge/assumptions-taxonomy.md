# Assumptions taxonomy for hypothesis refinement

Assumptions matter in PhyloCrates only insofar as they change the meaning, plausibility, or testability of the hypothesis. Do not turn this file into a study-design checklist.

## Biological comparability assumptions
- The focal trait has comparable biological meaning across the taxa being discussed.
- States or categories refer to homologous or meaningfully comparable phenomena.
- A proposed mechanism can plausibly act at the biological level named in the hypothesis.

## Historical assumptions
- Present-day similarity is not automatically treated as repeated evolutionary evidence.
- Similar trait combinations may reflect inheritance from a common ancestor rather than independent origins.
- Claims about historical order are distinguished from claims about present-day association.

## Causal assumptions
- Directionality is explicit when the hypothesis uses causal verbs such as causes, drives, promotes, constrains, or leads to.
- A proposed cause is not defined by the outcome it is supposed to explain.
- Potential common causes and reverse-direction explanations are acknowledged.

## Adaptationist assumptions
- Apparent functionality does not by itself establish that a trait evolved because of that function.
- Non-adaptive, historical, developmental, or contingent explanations are considered when plausible.

## Trait-definition assumptions
- Continuous versus categorical framing is biologically meaningful rather than chosen only for convenience.
- Thresholds or categories do not silently redefine the phenomenon being explained.
- Terms such as specialization, complexity, risk, adaptation, or conservatism are operationally understandable at the conceptual level.

## Scope assumptions
- The claim's taxonomic and temporal scope is explicit.
- Generalization beyond the focal clade or context is not assumed without justification.
- A species-level pattern is not silently generalized to within-species processes, or vice versa.

## Socratic prompts
Use selectively:
- “What has to be true for that explanation to make biological sense?”
- “Are you assuming these species represent repeated evolutionary events, or could the pattern have been inherited from one ancestor?”
- “Does your wording imply directionality? If so, what makes that direction biologically plausible?”
- “Would the hypothesis still make sense if the trait had no adaptive function?”
