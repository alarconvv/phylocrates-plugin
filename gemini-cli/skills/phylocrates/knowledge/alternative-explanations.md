# Alternative explanations

The purpose of alternatives is not to create a long list. It is to prevent a favored story from becoming the only explanation considered.

## Core classes

### 1. Focal mechanistic explanation
The explanation the researcher currently favors.

### 2. Non-adaptive explanation
The pattern could arise through drift, constraint, developmental correlation, pleiotropy, historical contingency, or another process not involving the proposed adaptive mechanism.

### 3. Shared-history explanation
Many observed species may inherit the trait combination from one ancestral event rather than representing repeated support for the proposed relationship.

### 4. Common-cause explanation
A third biological or environmental process could influence both focal variables.

### 5. Reverse-direction explanation
The proposed outcome may influence the proposed cause, or the historical ordering may be opposite to what the focal hypothesis assumes.

### 6. Reciprocal / feedback explanation
The relationship may be bidirectional rather than one-way.

### 7. Observation or classification artifact
Trait definitions, category boundaries, taxonomic practice, or observation processes could create the apparent pattern.

### 8. Ascertainment explanation
The pattern may be especially visible in the taxa for which data exist or for which the trait was studied.

### 9. Trait-independent macroevolutionary explanation
For claims linking a trait to lineage proliferation/extinction, diversification heterogeneity could be produced by another process unrelated to the focal trait.

## Elicit → challenge → supplement → synthesize

1. **Elicit** the researcher's alternatives first.
2. **Challenge** whether all alternatives share the same hidden assumption.
3. **Supplement** only the missing classes that are scientifically plausible.
4. **Synthesize** a compact set of genuinely distinct hypotheses.

## Quality check

Two alternatives are not genuinely distinct if they make the same biological claim using different terminology.

A useful alternative should differ in at least one of:
- causal direction;
- biological mechanism;
- historical origin;
- level of action;
- expected evolutionary ordering;
- source of the observed association;
- adaptive versus non-adaptive interpretation.
