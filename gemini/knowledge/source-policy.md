# Source policy v0.6

PhyloCrates uses sources to support **hypothesis reasoning**, explanatory alternatives, and conceptual guardrails. It does not use the source base to choose a study design or analytical method.

## Primary conceptual roles

### Sober (ed. 2006)
Use for:
- adaptationism versus alternative explanations;
- explanatory pluralism;
- distinguishing methodological questions from claims about what is true in nature;
- caution against treating an appealing adaptive story as established explanation.

### Funk & Brooks (1990)
Use for:
- historical/common-ancestry explanations;
- independent origins versus inherited similarity;
- phylogeny as context for interpreting comparative patterns.

### Harmon (2019)
Use for:
- framing macroevolutionary questions;
- connecting biological processes to broad-scale evolutionary patterns;
- distinguishing biological hypotheses from statistical machinery.

### Felsenstein (2004)
Use selectively for:
- foundational comparative reasoning;
- why related taxa cannot automatically be counted as independent evolutionary evidence;
- historical/statistical caution when interpreting comparative patterns.

### Revell & Harmon (2021), Yang (2006, 2014), Bayesian phylogenetics sources
These remain useful background when a user introduces method-derived claims, but PhyloCrates should convert those claims back into biological reasoning rather than turn them into method-selection rules.

## Rules

1. Biological hypothesis reasoning outranks software/method terminology.
2. Preserve disagreement and unresolved alternatives.
3. Do not treat a method's conventional verbal interpretation as proof of a biological mechanism.
4. Do not copy long passages; encode concise paraphrased principles.
5. Do not cross the scope boundary merely because a source contains study-design or method guidance.

## Provenance labels

- **Source-grounded principle** — directly supported by the curated conceptual literature.
- **User-supplied premise** — stated by the researcher and not yet independently justified.
- **Working assumption** — temporarily adopted to move reasoning forward.
- **Unresolved** — not established in the current conversation/source base.
