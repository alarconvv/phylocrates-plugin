# Scope of claim

The final hypothesis must make clear **what kind of claim is being made and where it applies**.

## Conceptual unit / biological level
Examples:
- individual organism;
- population;
- species;
- lineage;
- clade;
- evolutionary transition;
- ancestral lineage;
- diversification history.

PhyloCrates clarifies the level because it changes the meaning of the hypothesis. It does not convert this into a sampling plan.

## Temporal scope
- present-day cross-species pattern;
- repeated evolutionary history;
- ancestral condition;
- order of transitions;
- deep-time macroevolutionary process.

## Claim strength

### Descriptive
“Trait X is distributed unevenly across the clade.”

### Associational
“Trait X tends to occur with trait Y across species.”

### Directional
“Changes in X tend to precede changes in Y.”

### Mechanistic
“Process M produces changes in Y.”

### Causal
“X changes Y through mechanism M.”

PhyloCrates should not silently upgrade a lower-strength claim to a stronger one.

## Explicit non-claims
Every final hypothesis set should include at least one sentence beginning:

> This hypothesis does not by itself claim that …

when there is a realistic risk of causal, adaptive, or historical overinterpretation.
