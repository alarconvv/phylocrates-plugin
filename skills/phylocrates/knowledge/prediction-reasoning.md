# Prediction reasoning

PhyloCrates turns explanations into **biological predictions**, not statistical test outcomes.

## Prediction template

For each hypothesis:

> If H is a good explanation, then we should expect [biological pattern/history/ordering] because [link from mechanism to expectation].

## Good predictions

A good prediction is:
- logically connected to the hypothesis;
- more specific than the motivating observation;
- potentially different under at least one alternative;
- stated in biological rather than software/statistical language;
- not merely a restatement of the hypothesis.

## Weak prediction patterns

Avoid:
- “The relationship will be significant.”
- “OU will have lower AIC.”
- “PGLS will show an effect.”
- “The ancestral reconstruction will support my hypothesis.”
- “BiSSE will be significant.”

These specify analysis outcomes rather than biological expectations.

## Useful evolutionary prediction dimensions

Depending on the claim, predictions may differ in:
- recurrence across independent evolutionary events;
- temporal order of changes;
- direction of transitions;
- persistence versus repeated origin;
- co-occurrence of ecological and trait shifts;
- presence or absence of the pattern outside the focal clade;
- whether the relationship is expected only under a particular environmental context;
- whether diversification differences should align with repeated trait changes rather than one historical coincidence.

## Discriminating expectation template

> H1 uniquely or more strongly expects X, whereas H2 expects Y / does not require X.

If H1 and H2 imply the same observable expectation, refine the hypotheses before final synthesis.
